    Averages hide which windows a model actually wins. Each row pairs two models on every window and asks, with a Wilcoxon signed-rank test, whether one is consistently better.

    The natural gas question

    Timestral's point-forecast lead over AutoETS rests largely on one series. Leave natural gas out and the MASE gap closes; the probabilistic (WQL) gap does not.

    Do related series help?

    The pairs suite gives each foundation model a companion: Brent with WTI, EUR with JPY, the S&P 500 with VIX. +cov passes it as a past covariate; +joint forecasts it alongside the target. Only the target is scored.

    Chronos-2 runs past-only covariates through the same attention group as extra targets, so its +cov and +joint are the same computation; only +cov is shown. The S&P 500 + VIX pairs start in 1990 and leave room for 36 yearly windows, so this suite runs 36 rather than 48.

    Corrections: 6 windows to 48

    The first runs used 6 windows per series. Batching made 48 cheap, and several early claims did not survive. They are kept here, marked, not quietly edited.

        Method

        Enough to rerun it or to distrust it for the right reasons.

        Protocol
        Rolling origin. The last W × H points of each series form W back-to-back windows of horizon H (20 trading days for daily series, 12 months for monthly). Each model sees only the history before a window's cutoff. Statistical models refit on every window, on at most the last 1,024 points.
        Scores
        MASE: mean absolute error scaled by the in-sample seasonal-naive error. WQL: weighted quantile loss over the nine deciles. 80% coverage: share of outcomes inside the 10–90% band. A model's suite score is the geometric mean over series of its ratio to the random walk.
        Significance
        Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on per-window log MASE ratios. No multiple-comparison correction is applied to the p-values shown; read p near 0.02 with that in mind.
        Data
        11 public series from github.com/datasets, pinned by commit: S&P 500 level and log returns, CPI, 10-year Treasury yield and gold (monthly); VIX, WTI, Brent, Henry Hub natural gas, EUR and JPY per USD (daily). The S&P 500 and VIX are fetched on use rather than committed, since their owners restrict redistribution.
        Models
        Timestral 5M, 28M, 123M and 209M (pinned commit a534bdb); Chronos-2 small (28M) and base (120M), chronos-forecasting 2.3.2; statsforecast 2.0.1. All zero-shot with default settings. Torch 2.14.1 (CUDA 13) on an RTX 4090 under WSL2.
        Caveats
        One domain, mostly random-walk-like prices. Monthly windows reach back to 1978, which both foundation models may have seen in pretraining. Daily windows cover only late 2022 to October 2026.

        Terms

        The vocabulary above in a sentence or two each, with somewhere to read the long version.

        Models

        Random walk (naive)

        Forecast every future step as the last value seen. It is the baseline every score here is divided by, and on prices it is notoriously hard to beat.

        FPP3 §5.2: simple methods

        Historic average

        Forecast the mean of all history. It is a weak model for prices but the right baseline for returns, which are close to noise around a constant mean.

        FPP3 §5.2: the mean method

        AutoETS

        Exponential smoothing: a forecast is a weighted average of past values, with weights that decay the further back they go. ETS models the error, trend and seasonality, each as none, additive or multiplicative. "Auto" fits the candidate combinations and keeps the one with the best AICc.

        FPP3 ch. 8: ETS models · statsforecast AutoETS

        AutoARIMA

        ARIMA predicts a value from the series' own recent values (AR) and recent forecast errors (MA). It runs on a series differenced (I) until it has no trend. "Auto" searches the orders by AICc with the Hyndman–Khandakar algorithm. Refitting it on every window is most of the benchmark's run time.

        FPP3 ch. 9: ARIMA · statsforecast AutoARIMA

        Foundation model

        A neural network pretrained on a large, varied corpus of time series. It forecasts a series it has never seen by reading the history as context, with no fitting on that series.

        Ansari et al., Chronos (arXiv 2403.07815v3)

        Timestral

        Mistral AI's family of time series foundation models, at 5M, 28M, 123M and 209M parameters. Its weights are under a Modified MIT license that excludes large companies.

        Model card · mistralai/timestral

        Chronos-2

        Amazon's foundation model. It goes beyond single-series forecasting: group attention lets it read several related series and covariates at once. We run base (120M) and small (28M).

        Ansari et al., Chronos-2 (arXiv 2510.15821v1) · Model card

        Companion (+cov, +joint)

        A related series fed in alongside the target and cut at the same point, such as WTI for Brent or VIX for the S&P 500. With +cov it is a past-only covariate. With +joint both series are forecast together.

        ADR-0016: companion series

        Scores

        MASE

        Mean absolute scaled error: the forecast's mean absolute error divided by the in-sample error of a seasonal-naive forecast. Scaling makes it unit-free, so a series in dollars and one in percent points can sit in the same average. MASE is the point-forecast score.

        FPP3 §5.8: scaled errors · Hyndman & Koehler (2006)

        WQL

        Weighted quantile loss. Each model forecasts nine deciles, from 10% to 90%, not one number. WQL takes the pinball loss at each decile, divides it by the sum of the actual values, and averages over the deciles. It approximates CRPS, so it scores the whole forecast distribution: being sure and right, being wide when unsure. We use it because uncertainty is what the foundation models are built to forecast, and a point score alone would miss most of what they produce.

        FPP3 §5.9: quantile scores · AWS: wQL · Chronos paper: WQL and CRPS

        Relative score (rel MASE, rel WQL)

        A model's score on each series divided by the random walk's, combined across series with a geometric mean. With a geometric mean, a halving on one series and a doubling on another cancel out, and no single volatile series dominates. Below 1.00 beats the baseline.

        Fleming & Wallace (1986) · GIFT-Eval (arXiv 2410.10393v2)

        80% coverage

        The share of actual outcomes that land inside a model's 10–90% band. A calibrated model scores about 0.80. Above that its intervals are too wide (over-covering); below, too narrow and overconfident.

        FPP3 §5.5: prediction intervals · Gneiting et al. (2007)

        Direction

        The share of forecast steps whose up-or-down move from the last observation matches what happened. It is easy to read but blunt: it ignores how far the series moved.

        PIT

        The probability integral transform: where each actual falls within its forecast distribution, as a percentile. If forecasts are calibrated these percentiles spread evenly, so a histogram of them shows miscalibration at every level, not just at 80%.

        Gneiting et al. (2007)

        Protocol

        Rolling-origin backtest

        Cut the series at a point, forecast what follows, score it, then move the cut forward and repeat. It is cross-validation that respects time: no model ever sees the future it is scored on.

        FPP3 §5.10: time series cross-validation

        Window, cutoff, horizon

        A window is one cutoff plus the steps after it. The cutoff is the last point the model sees. The horizon is how many steps are forecast: 20 trading days for daily series, 12 months for monthly.

        tsfore glossary

        Zero-shot

        No training or fine-tuning on the series being forecast, and no tuned settings. Every model here runs on defaults.

        Chronos (arXiv 2403.07815v3)

        Leakage

        The test data, or data close to it, was in a model's training set, so its scores flatter it. The monthly windows reach back to 1978, which the foundation models may well have seen in pretraining.

        Wikipedia: leakage · fev-bench (arXiv 2509.26468v4)

        Statistics

        Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test

        It compares two models on the same windows. It ranks the per-window differences (here, log MASE ratios) by size and asks whether one side's wins outweigh the other's by more than chance would. It does not assume the errors are normally distributed.

        scipy.stats.wilcoxon · Wikipedia

        p-value

        How often a gap at least this lopsided would turn up if the two models were equally good. A small p says the gap is unlikely to be chance. It does not say how large the gap is.

        Wikipedia: p-value

        Bonferroni correction

        Run enough tests and something will look significant by luck. Bonferroni multiplies each p-value by the number of tests run, which keeps the chance of any false positive under the threshold. That is why the one companion that helped, at p = 0.017 among six comparisons, ends up near 0.10.

        Wikipedia: Bonferroni correction
