## Reading it

- **Update (2026-10-18), 48-window rerun, VERIFIED** (`finance-48w-2026-10-18.md`):
  - *Every Timestral size beats Chronos-2:* holds. 5M scores 0.869/0.766 against
    Chronos-2's 0.890/0.802, and 209M beats Chronos-2 window by window (p = 2e-5).
  - *Where Chronos-2 wins:* natgas is now a tie (0.644 against Timestral's 0.639), and
    EUR is a loss (1.032 against 1.008). The S&P 500 level remains a narrow win (5.95
    against 5.99). New: Chronos-2 wins on the 10-year yield (0.944 against 0.997).
  - *Calibration:* Timestral's coverage rises to 0.80–0.81 and Chronos-2's to 0.78.
    Chronos-2 remains too narrow.
- **Head-to-head:** every Timestral size, including 5M, beats Chronos-2 (120M) on both
  point and probabilistic error across this suite. Timestral-209M scores rel_MASE 0.866
  and rel_WQL 0.691; Chronos-2 scores 0.927 and 0.753. Chronos-2 still beats AutoETS on
  WQL but not on MASE, and Chronos-2-small barely beats the random walk on MASE (0.983).
- **Calibration** explains much of the WQL gap. Chronos-2's 80% bands cover 0.73–0.74,
  meaning they are too narrow. Timestral's cover 0.76–0.80.
- **Where Chronos-2 wins:** the S&P 500 level (6.79 against 7.43), natural gas (0.349,
  the only model below the random walk there) and EUR (1.024). It ties Timestral on S&P
  log returns (0.596 against 0.594).
- **Where it loses most:** CPI (1.84 against 1.17 for Timestral and 1.09 for AutoETS),
  oil (Brent 4.09 and WTI 3.45, both worse than the random walk) and VIX (1.10 against
  1.03).
- **Context length is not the explanation.** Chronos-2 caps context at 8,192 points and
  Timestral at 32,768. Only 4 daily series exceed 8,192 points (JPY at 13,980; VIX, WTI
  and Brent within 15%), and the biggest gap is on CPI, a 1,363-point monthly series that
  both models see in full.
- **Speed:** Chronos-2 (120M) takes 0.92 s per call against 0.33 s for Timestral-209M.
  Its default path is not tuned for batch-of-one calls.
  - **Correction (2026-10-18), VERIFIED:** these timings include lazy weight loading on
    the first window of every series. Warm, unbatched per-window times are 0.02–0.04 s
    for Chronos-2 and 0.035–0.08 s for Timestral (on Brent, 12 windows). Chronos-2 is the faster
    model, not the slower one. With batching, per-window times are 0.005–0.016 s for
    Chronos-2 and Timestral-28M/123M/209M (5M: 0.034 s). See
    `docs/benchmarks/batching-2026-10-18.md`. Accuracy figures are unaffected.
- **Caveats:**
  - This is one suite of 11 mostly random-walk-like finance series, with 6 recent
    windows each. Published broad benchmarks (GIFT-Eval, fev-bench) rank Chronos-2
    highly, and this result does not generalise beyond the suite.
  - The Chronos-2 adapter sorts each step's quantiles (it can emit crossed ones). That
    only reorders values and never widens the bands.
  - Neither model uses covariates here, although both support them.
