## Reading it

- **Update (2026-10-18), 48-window rerun, VERIFIED** (`finance-48w-2026-10-18.md`):
  - *Every size beats AutoETS:* holds on averages (0.848–0.869 against 0.893). On
    points, though, the lead is mostly natural gas: without it, 209M and AutoETS tie
    on rel_MASE (0.876 against 0.881), and the window-level test gives p = 0.12. The
    WQL lead survives without natgas (0.789 against 0.821).
  - *Bigger is better:* only 5M is distinguishable. 28M, 123M and 209M tie (28M
    against 209M: p = 0.35).
- **Overall:** every Timestral size beats every baseline on both point and probabilistic
  error. The point-accuracy edge is small: rel_MASE 0.866 for 209M against 0.895 for
  AutoETS. The probabilistic edge is large: rel_WQL is 0.69–0.71 for every size against
  0.83 for AutoETS, about 15% lower quantile loss.
- **Scaling:** improvement with size is monotone but shallow (5M 0.890 → 209M 0.866 MASE).
  Timestral-28M gets most of the benefit at a third of 209M's latency.
- **Calibration:** 80% coverage is 0.76–0.80. 209M (0.795) matches AutoETS (0.803), and
  its bands are tighter, which is why its WQL is much lower.
- **Market prices:** the biggest win is VIX (1.03 against 1.26 for the random walk), but
  `historic_average` (0.893) still beats it there. VIX mean-reverts, and a long-run mean
  captures that better. Brent shows a small edge (about 3%). WTI, EUR and JPY stay at
  random-walk level, which is the expected result.
- **Returns:** on `sp500_monthly_logret` Timestral (0.594) is the first model to beat
  `historic_average` (0.633).
- **Where classical still wins:** gold (AutoARIMA 2.63 against 3.03) and CPI (AutoETS
  1.09 against 1.17), both smooth, trending monthly macro series.
- **Speed:** `seconds` is wall time per forecast call. On the 4090 it is 0.12–0.35 s for
  every Timestral size, against 4.5 s for AutoARIMA on the CPU. The 5M figure likely
  includes first-call CUDA warm-up.
  - **Correction (2026-10-18), VERIFIED:** these timings include lazy weight loading on
    the first window of every series. Warm, unbatched per-window times are 0.02–0.04 s
    for Chronos-2 and 0.035–0.08 s for Timestral (on Brent, 12 windows). Chronos-2 is the faster
    model, not the slower one. With batching, per-window times are 0.005–0.016 s for
    Chronos-2 and Timestral-28M/123M/209M (5M: 0.034 s). See
    `docs/benchmarks/batching-2026-10-18.md`. Accuracy figures are unaffected.
- **Caveats:** 6 windows per series is a small, recent-regime sample. Absolute MASE on
  `sp500_monthly` is inflated by 1871-era prices. Timestral runs zero-shot, univariate,
  with default settings (symmetric averaging on, full context).
